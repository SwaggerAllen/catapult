## Summary

Rows classified: 44 (rows 1, 12–54).

- **ENGINE — 6:** 37, 39, 43, 46, 52, 54. Of these, only 43, 52 and 54 depend on the rule as stated. 37 needs the weaker "every `status:` kind is an existing atom" (and `review:` resolution only on the decline path); 39 needs only "a population anchor carries `flow:`"; 46 needs only "at least one anchor per sub-array".
- **LOADER-ONLY — 11:** 13, 16, 24, 25, 36, 40, 42, 44, 45, 48, 53. Notable: 24/48 are re-derived at the command edge from `throwback_targets/3`, so the declared `throwback:` is only a default; 42/53's namespace resolution of `blocks:` has no runtime counterpart at all (`ContainerQueues` matches bare kinds); 44's `entry:` has no reader.
- **HABIT — 18:** 1, 14, 15, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, 26, 27, 28, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 50.
- **NEGATIVE — 9:** 12, 17, 22, 29, 38, 41, 47, plus the two derivations 49 and 51 (runtime API, not checks).

**HABIT rules, named:** unknown-field rejection and its four restatements (1, 19, 21, 30, 35); `pending` placement (14, 15); `depth:` shape (18); critique adjacency (20); gate escalation/forward-exit shape (23); both skeleton backbones (26, 27); merge-after-reconcile (28); the two opt-in checks (31, 32); naming discipline (33); the runtime-dialect refusal (34); and the explicit-`throwback:` requirement for an ungrouped review (50), which the loader does not implement anyway.

Two things worth flagging beyond the classification. First, the skeleton-backbone checks (26, 27) protect nothing the dispatchers read: `ContainerLifecycle` iterates whatever array it is given and closes at `terminal` *or* at end-of-array; `FeatureLifecycle.Sequence` reads `checks`/`merge` only as a boundary and tolerates their absence. Second, the sub-array anchor count (46) is the one structural rule whose violation takes down every reader at once, because `Type.namespaced_positions/1` is the shared computation both axes and both LiveViews go through.
